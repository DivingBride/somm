# Table 6 - Physiological Effects

| Compound / Ingredient | Effect Category | Effect on the Palate | Foods / Sources | Effect on Wine Perception | Wine Service Implication | Chapter Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Menthol | COLD-TASTER | Activates cold receptors (8-28C); creates sustained sensation of coolness in the mouth | Mint / Basil / Lemon balm / Fresh coriander / Fresh fennel / Some root vegetables | Amplifies acidity and bitterness in wine; dry nervy Riesling becomes biting and explosive; wine seems colder than it actually is | Serve wine 1-2C warmer than usual; choose wines with moderate acidity (Albarino, Verdejo, Gruner Veltliner) not extremely nervy high-acid ones; avoid ice-cold serving temperature | Cold-Tasting Foods |
| Estragole | COLD-TASTER | Activates cold receptors (8-28C); cold sensation especially when food eaten raw | Tarragon / Green basil / Apples (raw) / Chinese star anise | Same as menthol: amplifies acidity, bitterness, and cold perception in wine; sweet wines seem less sweet than they actually are | Same as menthol; a dish entirely of cold-tasting ingredients with a very sweet wine will seem more acidic and bitter than intended; serve wine warmer | Cold-Tasting Foods / Mint & Sauvignon Blanc |
| Cineol / Eucalyptol | COLD-TASTER | Creates camphor and eucalyptus cool sensation in mouth | Rosemary / Eucalyptus / Bay leaf / Cardamom / Ginger | Cool sensation; reinforces perception of freshness; amplifies cold in wine | Pair with terpenic wines (Riesling, Gewurztraminer) whose terpenes amplify the cooling rather than clash; avoid ice-cold wines | Rosemary / Ginger |
| Camphene | COLD-TASTER | Gripping cool sensation of camphor and eucalyptus | Ginger / Rosemary | Reinforces cold perception in palate | Avoid very high-acid wines served ice-cold; the cold from the food combined with cold from serving temperature can become overwhelming | Ginger / Cold-Tasting Foods |
| Capsaicin | HEAT-MAKER | Activates heat receptors; causes burning sensation; promotes endorphin release; temporarily inflames mouth lining, increasing sensitivity of taste buds and mucous membranes | All chili peppers / Paprika / Pimenton (milder) | Amplifies perception of wine's alcohol and tannins (both also generate heat); makes wine seem much more alcoholic than it is; amplifies tannin bitterness | CRITICAL: Serve reds very cool (57-59F / 14-15C); same red wine at 64F will seem unbearably hot; sweet wines reduce capsaicin fire; dry abrasive foods (rice, beans) distract the palate | Capsaicin |
| Dihydrocapsaicin | HEAT-MAKER | Similar to capsaicin; also found in cinnamon at trace levels | Chili peppers / Cinnamon (trace) | Similar to capsaicin; cinnamon's dihydrocapsaicin can amplify apparent heat in pairing with high-alcohol wine | For cinnamon-heavy dishes choose wines without excessive alcohol; terpenic wines (Gewurztraminer) manage cinnamon heat through aromatic bridge rather than structural clash | Capsaicin / Cinnamon |
| Alcohol (Ethanol) | HEAT-MAKER | Generates sensation of heat in mucous membranes; enhances perception of sweetness and body | Wine itself / All alcoholic beverages | With capsaicin: mutual amplification of heat -- dangerous combination if wine served too warm. With cold-tasting foods: acts as a buffer against the cold sensation | When pairing with cold-tasting foods a wine rich in alcohol can serve as a buffer to the cold sensation; conversely avoid high-alcohol wines with very spicy dishes served warm | Capsaicin / Cold-Tasting Foods |
| Glycyrrhizic Acid (Black Licorice Extract) | TANNIN SOFTENER | Saponin emulsifier; directly softens tannins in red wine; extends anethole flavor persistence | Black licorice extract (concentrate) / Licorice root / Pastis (added during production) | Literally softens the tannic structure of reds; qualitatively augments small red wines and literally amplifies fine red wines -- Chartier; also extends anethole flavor persistence | Add black licorice extract to dishes paired with young tannic GSM blends or young Syrah; extends the wine's mid-palate and softens tannins; tarragon-based dishes also have a softening effect | Mint & Sauvignon Blanc |
| Anethole (via tarragon or anise-dominant dishes) | TANNIN SOFTENER | Anise-dominant dishes have a softening and lengthening effect on tannic red wines | Tarragon / Star anise / Pastis-based dishes / Fennel (moderate) | Extends flavor persistence of tannic red wines; softens rough tannin structure | When pairing young tannic reds with anise-family dishes expect tannins to integrate more smoothly than with neutral dishes; deliberately pair young Syrah with tarragon dishes to benefit from this | Mint & Sauvignon Blanc |
| Umami (Amino Acids / Glutamates) | TANNIN SOFTENER AND FLAVOR ENHANCER | Creates fullness and volume on the palate; lingering persistence; partially buffers tannin harshness | Aged beef / Parmigiano-Reggiano / Mushrooms / Kombu seaweed / Soy sauce / Fermented ingredients | Amplifies wine's mid-palate weight and apparent fullness; amino acids from sur lie aging also soften wine | With umami-rich foods wine's body seems greater; choose wines that match the expected amplified body rather than the dish's actual body alone; explains why aged beef calls for more voluminous wines | Beef / Cheese |
| Maltol | FLAVOR AMPLIFIER | Enhances and amplifies surrounding flavors; naturally sweetens perception without adding sugar | Oak wood / Larch / Pine / Licorice root / Roasted malt / Beer / Scotch | Wines rich in maltol (from oak) seem more aromatic and flavorful alongside foods that also contain maltol; amplification is mutual and additive | When a dish contains maltol-rich ingredients (maple syrup, caramel, toasted almonds) pair with wines from the same family; the flavor amplification works in both directions | Oak & Barrels / Maple Syrup |
| Cyclotene | FLAVOR AMPLIFIER | Very powerful aroma halfway between maple and licorice; boosts surrounding aromatic compounds | Roasted almonds / Coffee / Cocoa / Roasted fenugreek / Maple syrup / Licorice root | Coffee combined with cyclotene acts as a flavor enhancer for maple notes in wine; cyclotene in wine (from oak) responds to cyclotene in food by amplifying aromatic presence | Combining maple syrup with coffee in a dish creates compounding amplification when paired with oak-aged wines; Chartier explicitly notes cyclotene as a flavor enhancer compound | Maple Syrup / Oak & Barrels |
| Coffee (Ethyl Cyclotene) | FLAVOR AMPLIFIER | Ethyl cyclotene in coffee acts as flavor enhancer for all surrounding compounds; amplifies richness and aromatic depth | Coffee (roasted beans) / Ground coffee used in cooking | Coffee's ethyl cyclotene amplifies maple, caramel, and oak aromatic notes in wines; gives more presence and amplitude to the flavors of maple syrup -- Chartier | Use coffee in a marinade or sauce when pairing with barrel-aged wines; coffee amplifies the wood and caramel notes in wine; explains the classic Scotch and espresso pairing logic | Maple Syrup |
| Alpha-Pinene (iodine sublimation) | FLAVOR AMPLIFIER (reactive chemistry) | Alpha-pinene reacts with iodine, sublimating (intensifying) the iodine flavor; makes iodine note more powerful and pronounced | Saffron (minor) / Juniper berries / Rosemary / Pine needles / Seaweed / Oysters and scampi (iodine-tasting) | Riesling's alpha-pinene will intensify the iodine character in oysters; creates a stronger and more harmonious union; alpha-pinene is soluble in alcohol and fat not water | When pairing iodine-tasting seafood with Riesling expect iodine to seem MORE pronounced not less -- this is a feature not a problem; alpha-pinene perfumes the iodine note and makes it more elegant | Saffron / Rosemary |
| Capsaicin (endorphin trigger) | FLAVOR AMPLIFIER (neurochemical) | Promotes release of endorphins; creates pleasure response; temporarily increases sensitivity of taste buds, amplifying all subsequent flavor perceptions | All chili peppers | After a spicy dish subsequent flavors including wine register more intensely because taste bud sensitivity has been temporarily elevated | Use deliberately: a mildly spicy amuse-bouche before a wine-paired course primes the palate for greater aromatic sensitivity; a sip of wine after a spicy bite can seem more aromatic than usual | Capsaicin |
| Sugar / Residual Sugar in wine | FLAVOR MODIFIER (heat counter) | Directly counters the burning sensation of capsaicin; dilutes capsaicin's heat receptor activation | Sugar / Residual sugar in sweet wines | A sweet wine with a spicy dish seems less sweet than it actually is; sugar acts as a fire extinguisher for capsaicin | Sweet wines (Gewurztraminer Vendanges Tardives, off-dry Riesling, Sauternes, Banyuls) manage very spicy dishes better than dry wines; residual sugar is a direct chemical counter to capsaicin heat | Capsaicin |
